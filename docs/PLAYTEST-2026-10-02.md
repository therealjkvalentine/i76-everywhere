## Why the jumps and the body roll work now (answer to 5, 6, 7)

Stock at 20 fps does not step physics at 50 ms: the GetTickCount clock hands the stepper 46.9 ms frames 80 % of the
time and 31.2 ms the rest, mean 41.7 ms. A 60 fps game stepping at 16.7 ms integrates the same forces with three
times the resolution, and the engine's tuning (ramp impulses, suspension response, roll damping) was done against the
coarse step, so jumps fell short and the body felt quick. `I76_FIXED_STEP=24` makes the physics always step in
41.67 ms slices, the measured mean of stock play, whatever the frame rate; `I76_ENGINE_DT_FIX` gives the engine model
its 0.05 s substep back; `I76_RENDER_INTERP` draws the car and camera between the last two physics poses so the 24 Hz
step is not visible. The first attempt used 40 steps/s (25 ms) and the jumps fell short, which is how the number was
found (music-fix/README.md switch table, i76-map captures/014-framerate). Gravity itself was never the problem: it is
dt-correct in stock (ENGINE-REFERENCE.md section 4). "Sketchy at 20 fps" is the stock clock's 47/31 ms jitter; the
fixed step removes the jitter too, which is why it feels *more* reliable than stock 20 rather than merely equal.

## 120 fps (answer to 10)

The ceiling was not the panel, DWM, `FPSLimit` or vsync: ZGLIDE.DLL asks dgVoodoo for a 60 Hz refresh in
`grSstWinOpen` and dgVoodoo paces windowed presents at the requested rate. The proxy now substitutes the refresh code
(`I76_GLIDE_REFRESH=120`). Measured: 120.1 fps, frame dt 8.34 ms (sd 0.06), physics still 24 steps/s, hood view and
binoculars fine. Implications: physics, AI and input run exactly as at 60 (the sim is on the fixed step); 4 of every
5 frames are interpolated poses, so smoothness improves and nothing else changes; GPU headroom at 1800 m and 8x AA is
the practical limit. Not yet done at 120: the Mission 5 jump, the collision sweep test, a human pass.
